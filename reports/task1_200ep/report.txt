Task 1: Frequency-Selective Residual Decay — audited experiment report

The result supports the main Task 1 hypothesis in one controlled setting. With
200 epochs of MAE pretraining on the approved CMC-160 ImageNet-100 release,
beta=0.5 improved linear-probe accuracy by 7.7 percentage points and KNN accuracy
by 11.0 points over original fixed decay. Deep patch features also contained a
substantially smaller high-frequency energy share. This is evidence from one
seed and one evaluation subset, rather than a general proof or a complete paper
reproduction.

Final recorded results:
Method                         Train MAE   Validation MAE   KNN      Linear probe
Standard shortcuts             0.379878    0.409020         28.5%    34.7%
Fixed decay, a_min=.7           0.381645    0.410559         37.6%    44.6%
Frequency-selective, beta=.5    0.384452    0.412325         48.6%    52.3%

The active method gains 17.6 linear-probe points and 20.1 KNN points over the
standard control. Against fixed decay it classifies 145 previously incorrect
validation images correctly while losing 68 previously correct images: a net
77 more correct predictions on the same 1000 images. Classification accuracy
therefore improved while reconstruction loss increased slightly: validation
MAE was 0.43% higher than fixed decay. Better reconstruction was not demonstrated.

Every model used the same repository architecture, including encoder-to-decoder
skip connections and initialization. The control is the same architecture with
all encoder shortcut multipliers set to one, rather than an unrelated stock MAE.
All have 117038848 trainable parameters. The active method adds none.

Common settings were ViT-B/16, 224x224 model inputs, a 12-block 768-channel
encoder, 75% masking, normalized pixel reconstruction loss, AdamW, weight decay
0.05, peak learning rate 0.00015, 20 warmup epochs and cosine learning-rate decay.
All models started from scratch with seed 0 and no pretrained checkpoint.
Physical batch size was 32, with eight batches accumulated per optimizer update:
the effective batch was 256 for every method. Precision was FP16 mixed precision.
The GPU was an H200 with a 7 GiB PyTorch allocator limit. The exact launch commands
and source run paths are in commands.txt and audit.json. Training source commit:
6b3d9996c492bc544b62b170a6155c3c35f53b88 (clean checkout at launch).

The approved dataset was clane9/imagenet-100, revision
0519dc2f402a3a18c6e57f7913db059215eee25b, with 126689 training and 5000 validation
images in 100 CMC classes. The source resized images to 160 pixels on the shorter
side before our 224-pixel training crops. Every original dataset shard checksum
was verified, and the class list, counts and label mapping are tracked in
MAE/configs/. With drop_last=True, 126688 training images were processed per epoch.
This dataset was approved as a new exploratory comparison: the teammate's exact
baseline commands, class list and checkpoint provenance remain unverified.

MAE validation used all 5000 validation images, fixed resize/center-crop transforms
and mask seed 12345, once every five epochs. Each method completed 40 validation
passes. All recorded losses were finite, and every successive validation loss
was lower than the previous one. Each method attempted 99000 optimizer updates;
33 FP16 overflow updates were automatically skipped, leaving exactly 98967 actual
updates in every method. The skipped updates were recorded, not counted as steps.

Classification evaluation used exactly the same 1000 training images and 1000
validation images for every method, with ten images per class in each subset.
The image indices and labels were compared directly across saved caches. Encoder
features were extracted without masking in batches of two, then cached on CPU
and disk. Pooling follows the repository's custom MAE evaluator: average all final
raw tokens INCLUDING CLS, without adding an encoder output normalization.
KNN used cosine-normalized features, k=20 and exponential vote temperature 0.07.
The linear classifier was trained on frozen cached features for 200 epochs using
AdamW, batch 256, initial learning rate 0.01, and a cosine schedule. Its loss was
logged separately from MAE loss. Saved classifier weights and cached validation
features reproduced all three reported accuracies, and recomputed KNN matched too.
The 1% chance level for balanced 100-class classification is far below all scores,
but these are small-label-subset results, not full-data benchmark accuracies.

The shortcut is a*X + beta*(1-a)*L, where L is the visibility-normalized 5x5 uniform
local average. Writing X=L+(X-L) gives:
    [a + beta*(1-a)]*L + a*(X-L).
Thus beta=.5 preserves the local-average term with coefficient (1+a)/2, while
the detail term retains coefficient a. This algebra explains the intended change;
under an irregular MAE visibility mask, L is a masked local average rather than a
perfect Fourier low-frequency projection. Visible tokens are scattered into their
original grid positions, features and visibility are averaged together, and the
result is gathered back in the visible-token order. CLS retains the scalar
shortcut, and the local average is recomputed for the MLP shortcut. The decoder,
learned branches, initialization and reconstruction objective are preserved.

Frequency analysis used the same 200 complete, unmasked validation images for
all methods and all 12 encoder blocks. CLS was excluded. Per-channel spatial
means were removed, and a 2D orthonormal FFT was applied to the 14x14 patch grid.
The fixed high-frequency cutoff was radial frequency above 0.25 cycles per patch.
The high-frequency fraction divides summed high-frequency energy by all non-DC
spatial energy across the evaluated images/channels. Absolute energy is averaged per image and summed over channels and frequency bins.
Low-frequency energy below includes non-DC frequencies only; the spatial mean/DC
component is excluded.

Final encoder block (12):
Method                 HF share    HF energy    LF energy    Total variation
Standard               54.46%      112172.92    93790.86     1.805424
Fixed decay            42.09%       16062.49    22098.97     0.259176
Frequency-selective    14.46%        7181.05    42473.92     0.120050

Compared with fixed decay, the active method reduced high-frequency share by
27.63 percentage points (65.6% relative), absolute high-frequency energy by 55.3%,
and mean squared neighbouring-patch variation by 53.7%. Its low-frequency energy
increased by 92.2%, and total non-DC energy increased by 30.1%. The lower HF share
therefore was not caused by uniformly shrinking every feature. These are raw
learned-feature units and support the expected spectral effect; they do not prove
that frequency suppression is the sole cause of the classification gain. The
active method has larger variation in some early blocks, so the result is not
that every layer is smoother. The clearest suppression appears in deeper blocks.
All depth measurements are in depth_metrics.csv and depth_comparison.png.

Effective rank was measured as covariance singular-value entropy using natural
logarithms, WITHOUT exponentiation. Features were CLS vectors from the same 200
images. Rows represented samples and columns represented 768 channels. Each row
was centered across channels, and sample-by-sample covariance was Xc*Xc.T/(768-1).
Normalized nonnegative covariance eigenvalues equal its normalized singular values.
Final CLS rank entropy was 2.546771 (standard), 3.187825 (fixed), and 2.966075
(active). These values are entropies, not literal counts of feature dimensions.
The active method is lower than fixed decay but higher than standard; classification
accuracy is therefore NOT monotonically ordered by lower rank across these runs.
We did not confirm a universal claim that lower effective rank means better
representations. Entropy also generally grew toward the last encoder blocks,
so rank contraction across depth was not demonstrated. Rank describes CLS while classification pools all tokens, and
frequency analysis describes patch tokens; these feature choices must stay explicit.

Runtime including each run's setup and final evaluation was approximately
6h38m16s (standard), 6h44m05s (fixed), and 9h42m50s (active). The complete queue
finished in 23h05m23s, within the requested 24-hour budget. The active method took
44.2% longer than fixed decay. Peak PyTorch allocated memory was 4.207, 4.207 and
5.032 GiB, respectively; peak reserved memory was 4.643, 4.643 and 5.541 GiB.
The active method added about 19.6% peak allocated memory. Those are measured
PyTorch peaks, not continuous whole-process GPU-memory samples. Observed device
usage during the active run was about 6.3 GiB. No RTX 3070 run has been performed:
an H200 allocator cap is evidence for the memory budget, not hardware certification.

The required small implementation checks passed for beta=0 recovery, unit
multipliers, constant visible features, hidden-patch isolation, token order, spatial
positions, CLS, gradients, unchanged parameter count, finite forward/backward and
settings/output restoration. As an additional report audit, full TRAINED ViT-B
weights were loaded into the pre-change model and the current model with beta=0.
Two explicitly synthetic 224x224 inputs and identical masks gave exact equality
for encoder outputs, masks, reconstruction loss and unmasked features under the
same attention backend. Unit multipliers also recovered the same-architecture
standard shortcut with trained weights. These checks are synthetic correctness
evidence and are not ImageNet performance results.

The paper's reported ImageNet-100 protocol uses 800 pretraining epochs, effective
batch 1024, peak learning rate .0006 and 40 warmup epochs. Its linear probe uses
batch 1024 for 200 epochs. It reports 76.5% for the 768-channel/12-block standard
control and 81.8% at a_min=.7. Our shorter training, resized source images and
classification subset protocol prevent a direct numerical reproduction claim.
The lower learning rate in our run has the same learning-rate-to-batch ratio.
Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2404.10947v5 (Table 3 and Appendix A).

Recommended defensible claim:
“In one matched 200-epoch MAE experiment on the approved CMC-160 ImageNet-100
release, visibility-aware frequency-selective shortcut decay with beta=.5 improved
linear-probe accuracy by 7.7 percentage points and KNN accuracy by 11.0 points over
fixed decay on identical balanced 1000-image evaluation subsets. It also reduced
final-layer high-frequency energy share from 42.1% to 14.5% while retaining more
low-frequency energy, at a 44% runtime cost and about 20% extra peak allocated
memory. Multiple seeds and full-data evaluation are needed to establish robustness.”
